# Good questions. Thoughtful answers.

## Does playing help my brain?

A word puzzle gives you a chance to recall words, weigh clues, and try a different approach. That little moment of active thinking is what Knistertap is made for.

Whether that improves everyday memory or long-term brain health is a separate question. The National Institute on Aging cautions that evidence from specific cognitive-training studies does not establish the same benefits for commercial brain-training apps. Knistertap’s games have not been clinically validated.

[National Institute on Aging · Cognitive health](https://www.nia.nih.gov/health/brain-health/cognitive-health-and-older-adults)

## Why a puzzle break instead of doomscrolling?

Sometimes it feels good to give your attention a small, deliberate task. A puzzle is one option; a book, a conversation, or a walk can be another.

A 2024 survey of 800 university students in Iran and the US linked doomscrolling — repeatedly consuming negative news — with existential anxiety. It did not prove causation, study all social-media use, or test whether games reverse these effects.

[Shabahang et al. · 2024 study](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.chbr.2024.100438)

## Does staying mentally active matter more as we age?

Staying curious and connected matters throughout life. WHO identifies social isolation and cognitive inactivity as dementia risk factors. Dementia is more common after 65, but there is no established cutoff at 45 when everyone suddenly needs more stimulation.

A 2022 observational study followed 146,651 adults aged 60+ and linked more TV viewing with higher dementia risk. It cannot prove TV causes dementia or that replacing it with a game prevents it. Reading, chess, conversation, and movement offer different ways to make room for an engaged day.

[WHO · Dementia](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/dementia) · [Raichlen et al. · PNAS, 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9436362/)
